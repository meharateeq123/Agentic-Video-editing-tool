When I write Short scripts from a long ManimGL film, each one needs a real mathematical point that the viewer comes away understanding: a why, a mechanism, or a counterintuitive result they can check. A plain anecdote or a one-line fact isn't enough.

**Why:** on 2026-09-29 the user said the A&W burger short ("⅓ beats ¼") was too simple, and asked that future scripts not be simple and have a clear purpose ("maksad"). The Euler quarter-turn short (why e^(iπ) = −1) and the Möbius cut shorts are closer to what they want.

**Length:** no more than 20 seconds (user, 2026-09-29). At the Josh ElevenLabs voice's pace, about 0.4 s/word with pauses, that's roughly 45 words, including "Full video on the channel." Depth has to come from picking one sharp idea, not from more words.

**Pull to the long film:** the user said on 2026-09-29 "short itna interesting hona chahiye k log long dekhne par majboor ho jain": the short has to make people need the long video. So the short pays off one idea fully, then its last line before "Full video on the channel" opens a bigger question that only the long film answers. Make it specific ("but why does the tower always end in 7?"), never a vague "learn more".

**Closing line:** from the next short on, every short ends with "Watch the <topic> video to see how." (user's exact wording, 2026-09-29). Swap in the topic from that script, e.g. "Watch the Euler video to see how." The end HUD matches: "WATCH THE" / "<TOPIC> VIDEO".

The delivered Laplace shorts keep their old ending. The user said to leave them as they are.

History: "Full video on the channel." was replaced by "The answer's in the full video." That was rejected on 2026-09-29 after the Laplace shorts were delivered, and the user picked "Watch the full video to see how." with the topic name added ("laplace ki thi to Laplace likh dena").

**How to apply:**
- A short script needs three parts: a hook that sets up a question or surprise, the actual reasoning shown step by step (something the animation can prove), and a payoff line that lands the insight.
- Prefer the long film's "why" sections over its story or trivia sections. Cross-multiplying, common denominators, i as rotation, and the tower endings are good sources; a pure anecdote isn't.
- Pitch it as "one idea the viewer can repeat to a friend".
