One point. That's the entire margin separating the two best drivers in NASCAR right now, heading into a track that has produced the closest finish in Cup Series history and a late-race incident that cost one of them a win just last year.
Here's exactly how tight this championship battle has become, and why Kansas could blow it wide open.
Kyle Larson leads Denny Hamlin by exactly one point heading into Sunday's Hollywood Casino 400 at Kansas Speedway, the fourth race of NASCAR's ten-race Chase postseason.
It gets tighter than that. Just 17 points separate the top four drivers in the entire standings.
Under the current Chase format, drivers received predetermined bonus points based on their regular season finish, added once after a single reset, with no further resets for the rest of the playoffs. That means every position gained or lost in these early rounds carries real weight, and nobody can be mathematically eliminated this weekend at Kansas or even next weekend at Las Vegas.
Larson's path to the points lead started with a result that had been a long time coming. Two weekends ago, he won at World Wide Technology Raceway at Gateway, ending a 52-race winless streak and getting his number 5 Hendrick Motorsports Chevrolet back to victory lane for the first time in over a year.
He followed that up at Bristol with a runner-up finish, decided by one of the tightest margins of the season. In a late-race battle for position, Larson passed Carson Hocevar for second place in a drag race to the line that was settled by less than one-hundredth of a second, while Joey Logano went on to win the race outright.
That second-place finish at Bristol was enough to push Larson past Hamlin atop the standings, marking the first time all season a non-Toyota driver has led the points. Tyler Reddick had led after the first 17 races of the year, and Hamlin had led the 11 races after that. Larson becoming the new leader broke that run entirely.
Speaking after Bristol, Larson credited the team's execution rather than outright speed. "I don't think we were a top-five car, but we did a great night executing tonight, and that's what it takes," he said. "We weren't too hot there in the second stage, and really the majority of the final stage, but I'm proud of the pit call and the fight we had to hang on there."
What makes this standings battle so interesting is that Hamlin has not actually finished higher than eighth in any of the first three playoff races, and he still sits just one point back. That gap exists because Hamlin entered the postseason with a substantial cushion, having won the regular season title by 89 points over Ryan Blaney, a lead that translated into a strong predetermined bonus heading into the Chase reset. Even with three modest playoff finishes, that cushion has kept him within a single point of the top spot.
Hamlin has also been direct about needing to find better form. After his quiet eighth-place finish at Bristol, he said plainly that he needed to "get his mojo back." Kansas gives him a real chance to do exactly that.
He holds the highest number of top-five finishes among active drivers at the track in the NextGen car era, and he led the most laps in both Kansas races held last year. If any track on the schedule suits a bounce-back run, it is this one.
Kansas Speedway has a recent history of producing exactly the kind of chaos that could flip this standings battle overnight. In May 2024, it hosted the closest finish in Cup Series history, with Larson edging Chris Buescher by just 0.001 seconds.
Last fall, contact between Hamlin and Bubba Wallace on the final lap opened the door for Chase Elliott to steal the win away from both of them.
Elliott returns to Kansas this weekend still seeking another breakthrough at a track where he holds the best average finish among active drivers with at least five career starts there, backed by two wins and 14 top-10 finishes in 21 starts, including last year's race.
Reddick also has recent Kansas success, having won the spring race at the track back in April. Between Elliott's track record, Reddick's recent win, and Hamlin's own strong history there, Larson is walking into a race where several drivers around him have specific reasons to believe they can take the moment for themselves.
Kansas is also the first of three consecutive 1.5-mile, intermediate-style tracks on the schedule, with Las Vegas and Charlotte following in the weeks ahead.
Larson enters this stretch with real momentum behind him, carrying three straight top-five finishes into Kansas and leading all playoff drivers in combined points across the first three postseason races. But with no elimination on the line this weekend, every driver near the top of the standings has an incentive to race for position rather than play it safe, which only raises the odds of exactly the kind of late-race contact that has defined Kansas in recent years.
A one-point gap at the top of a major championship standings is rare in any sport, and NASCAR's Chase format makes it even more consequential, since there is no further points reset to smooth things out later.
Larson arrives with momentum and a car that has executed under pressure in back-to-back races. Hamlin arrives with a proven record at this exact track and a clear need to reset his own season narrative. And Kansas itself arrives with a recent track record of deciding races by fractions of a second or a single late-race mistake.
Whatever happens on Sunday, the math is unforgiving. A single caution at the wrong moment, one aggressive restart, or one late-race incident like the one that cost Hamlin the win here last year, and the top of the championship standings could look completely different by the checkered flag.
With this little separating the leaders, Kansas is not just another race on the schedule. It is the first real test of whether Larson's new lead is built to last, or whether Hamlin, Elliott, or Reddick are about to make this points battle look a lot less settled than it does right now.
