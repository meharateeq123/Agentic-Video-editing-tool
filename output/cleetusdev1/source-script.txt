Cleetus McFarland just showed up somewhere NOBODY expected… and it has nothing to do with a racetrack. The crazy part? This reveal could say more about Cleetus' racing career than you might think. Because iRacing just dropped something featuring Cleetus, and there's a reason they chose THAT car. So what exactly did they reveal, and why could this be a bigger deal for Cleetus than it looks?
iRacing Studios released the third episode of its NASCAR 26 Dev Diary, a behind-the-scenes video series showing off features and content coming to the next entry in its NASCAR console game line. Buried among the gameplay footage was a screenshot fans immediately zeroed in on: Cleetus McFarland's No. 30 car, rendered in-game, sitting on the track at Talladega.
For most of the game's development cycle, there had been little confirmation either way about whether Cleetus would return. He'd already made his NASCAR gaming debut in last year's game, NASCAR 25, but nothing guaranteed he'd carry over into the sequel. This Dev Diary settled that question. He's in.
The car shown in the reveal wasn't just any version of his No. 30 Ford Mustang. It was the BaldEagle.com paint scheme, one of three different looks Cleetus ran during his 2026 season. And this particular scheme wasn't picked at random for the screenshot. It's the paint scheme he debuted at Talladega, in a race that turned into one of the biggest moments of his racing career so far.
Cleetus came into that Talladega race as a part-time driver with a reputation still being built. He left it just a few feet short of a win. Racing in the ARCA Menards Series, he ran at the front for a chance at his first career victory, only to lose at the line to Andy Jankowiak. Second place doesn't sound like a headline until you remember where Cleetus started. This was a YouTuber with millions of subscribers, running side-by-side with lifelong racers for a national series win, and coming up just short.
Putting that exact car, in that exact paint scheme, into the first real look at NASCAR 26 is not a coincidence. It's the game studio choosing to represent Cleetus with the moment that best captures how much progress he's actually made behind the wheel.
To understand why this moment carries weight, you have to go back to how Cleetus even got into a NASCAR game in the first place.
Real name Garrett Mitchell, Cleetus built his following making car videos on YouTube, where his channel has grown past 4.7 million subscribers and over 2 billion views. In 2025, he made the jump from internet personality to actual racer, signing on for a part-time ARCA Menards Series schedule with Rette Jones Racing. That same year, iRacing Studios made the surprising decision to include the entire ARCA Menards Series in NASCAR 25, the first time that series had ever appeared in a NASCAR video game. Cleetus was part of that rollout, listed alongside legitimate rising prospects in the sport.
That's an important detail. Cleetus wasn't added to the game as a novelty separate from real competition. He was added because he was already racing against those same drivers in real ARCA events, and the game simply reflected that.
Since that first ARCA season, Cleetus hasn't slowed down. His 2025 debut season included a Daytona start that ended early in a multi-car crash, but by the end of the year he'd posted two top-10 finishes, including a career-best result at Charlotte.
2026 pushed him even further. He continued running ARCA with Rette Jones Racing, delivered that near-miss runner-up finish at Talladega, and expanded into NASCAR's higher touring divisions for the first time. He made his Craftsman Truck Series debut at Daytona, and signed a limited deal with Richard Childress Racing to run select O'Reilly Auto Parts Series races, debuting at Rockingham. Neither of those first attempts produced strong finishes, but that was never really the point. Each one added another chapter to a racing journey that started with online videos and is now stretching across three different national touring series.
That growth is exactly why his in-game presence has become something worth tracking. A driver who started as a curiosity in one series is now a recurring name across the sport's development pipeline, both on real racetracks and inside the games that simulate them.
Here's where it gets interesting. The Dev Diary confirmed Cleetus is back in the ARCA lineup for NASCAR 26. What it didn't confirm is whether his role in the game could expand.
Given that Cleetus raced in three different NASCAR touring series in 2026, not just ARCA, there's a real possibility his in-game footprint grows to match. Nothing has been officially announced about him appearing in the Truck Series or O'Reilly Series modes of NASCAR 26. But the logic tracks. If the game is built to reflect the current season's rosters, and Cleetus is now a multi-series driver in real life, leaving him limited to just his ARCA car would undersell what he's actually been doing on track this year.
For now, what's confirmed is straightforward: his No. 30 ARCA Ford is in the game, in a paint scheme tied directly to his best on-track result of the year. Anything beyond that remains speculation, but it's speculation built on a clear pattern of his racing career expanding every single season.
On the real-world side, Cleetus still has one event left on his 2026 schedule, a fall Truck Series race at Talladega. Beyond that, he's already floated something much bigger. Following his runner-up finish at Talladega, Cleetus said he's considering a full-time ARCA Menards Series schedule for 2027, which would mean roughly 20 races in a single season, a massive jump from the handful of starts he's run in each of the past two years.
If that happens, it would mark the clearest signal yet that Cleetus isn't treating this as a side project. A driver who started with four ARCA starts in 2025 and expanded into three different national series in 2026 would be moving toward a genuine full-time racing schedule in 2027.
And if that schedule becomes real, expect the video game side of his story to keep following right along with it. Whatever NASCAR 26 ends up showing this year, it's likely just the current snapshot of a driver whose in-game presence has grown at the exact same pace as his real one.
For a YouTuber who started out flooding burnouts in a driveway, showing up in a big-budget NASCAR video game two years in a row is already a milestone. But based on everything Cleetus has done since his ARCA debut, this might not be the last surprise fans get from him, on the track or in the game.
