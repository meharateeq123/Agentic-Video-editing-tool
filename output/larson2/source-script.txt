Kyle Larson JUST Dropped a BOMBSHELL on Denny Hamlin!

Forty-nine points. That's how far behind Denny Hamlin Kyle Larson was on Sunday morning. By Sunday night, that number was nine. One race. One driver. A 40-point swing that just turned the entire championship picture upside down. Here's exactly how it happened, and why Denny Hamlin should be worried heading into Bristol.
Larson came into the Enjoy Illinois 300 sitting seventh in the Chase standings, 49 points behind Hamlin. After a rough start to the postseason and a winless streak that had stretched across 51 races, Larson wasn't just chasing a championship. He was chasing relevance in a title fight that seemed to be slipping further away every week.
That's the position he was racing from when the green flag dropped at World Wide Technology Raceway. Nobody watching that morning would have called him the favorite to walk away as Hamlin's biggest threat by the end of the day.
What followed was one of the wildest Cup Series races in recent memory. The Enjoy Illinois 300 produced a track-record 18 cautions and 87 caution laps, sending contender after contender to the garage. Brake rotors exploded on multiple cars. Championship hopefuls got collected in wrecks that had nothing to do with their own mistakes. The race needed two overtime restarts just to reach a finish.
In the middle of all that chaos, Larson found his way to the front. He led 80 laps, won Stage 2, and passed Joey Logano for the lead following a restart with 13 laps to go. He held on through the final overtime, crossing the line 0.333 seconds ahead of his own Hendrick Motorsports teammate, Alex Bowman, to end his 51-race winless streak.
It was his 33rd career Cup win, his first at Gateway, and his first since May of 2025. But the number that actually matters isn't the win total. It's what that win did to the standings.
Larson's victory, combined with the stage points and playoff points that came with it, vaulted him from seventh to second in the Chase standings in a single afternoon. He entered Gateway 49 points behind Hamlin. He left just nine points back.
That's a 40-point swing in one race. In a championship format where every point matters across a 10-race postseason, a gap that size closing in a single Sunday is the kind of shift that changes how the entire field has to race for the rest of the year. Larson isn't a long shot chasing the leader anymore. He's the closest thing to a direct threat Hamlin has in the standings.
Here's what makes this shift even more dangerous for Hamlin. He didn't have a bad race. While cars were spinning, crashing, and losing brakes all around him, Hamlin largely stayed out of trouble, finishing ninth after a problematic pit stop cost him track position late in the race. That's a solid, mistake-free day by any normal measure.
It just wasn't enough. Hamlin still leads the Chase standings, but his cushion went from a comfortable gap to a nine-point margin that could disappear in a single stage at the next race. He didn't lose ground because he did something wrong. He lost ground because the driver right behind him did something exceptional.
That's a much scarier position for a points leader to be in. A leader who gets passed because of his own mistake can fix the mistake. A leader who watches his margin evaporate because a rival simply performed at a higher level has no obvious fix at all, other than hoping it doesn't happen again.
Larson wasn't shy about what Sunday meant, both for his confidence and for what comes next. "We got our swagger back," he said in Victory Lane. Asked whether the win meant he was fully back to the level that won him championships in the past, Larson didn't overstate it, but he didn't downplay it either.
"I don't know if we're quite yet where we can go dominate like we have in the past," Larson said, "but a day like today definitely gives you confidence leading into a track where we know we can go dominate."
That's not just a driver enjoying a win. That's a driver pointing directly at the next race on the schedule and saying he expects to be even stronger there. And he backed up his mindset through the whole losing streak, too. "I haven't doubted myself; I haven't doubted our team," Larson said. "Maybe myself at times, but never my team."
A driver who kept that level of belief through 51 races without a win, and then delivered exactly when it mattered most, is not a driver who's likely to fade quietly back into the middle of the pack.
The Chase now moves to Bristol Motor Speedway for the Bass Pro Shops Night Race on Saturday, September 19th, a half-mile track that rewards precision and punishes even small mistakes far more severely than a track like Gateway. It's also, by Larson's own admission, a track he believes his team can dominate.
There's one more detail that raises the stakes even further. Behind Larson and Hamlin, the field is far from settled. Christopher Bell sits third, 18 points back, still very much alive in the title fight. Ryan Blaney dropped to fourth after a brake failure ended his Gateway race early, now 33 points behind. Under this year's Chase format, there's no elimination cutoff looming at Bristol, which means every one of these drivers stays in the fight regardless of how they finish. But that format detail doesn't make the points any less real. It just means the pressure builds gradually instead of all at once.
For Hamlin, that's cold comfort. He's still the championship leader. But the driver now sitting nine points behind him just went from a name buried in the middle of the standings to the single biggest threat in the field, at a track that driver has already said he expects to dominate.
Nine points is nothing in a 10-race championship. It can disappear in half a stage, or it can hold for weeks if Hamlin's team responds the way they have all season. What's changed is the pressure. Before Gateway, Hamlin was racing with room to breathe. After it, he's racing with Kyle Larson in his mirror, fresh off ending a year-long drought, walking into a track he's already called a place where his team can dominate.
The next eight races will decide whether Sunday was the moment Larson officially took over this championship fight, or just one great day in the middle of a much longer battle. But after a 40-point swing in a single afternoon, one thing is already certain. Denny Hamlin knows exactly who he has to watch now.
