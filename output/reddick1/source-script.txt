The NASCAR Chase just began, and one driver already has a track record that should worry the other 15. Tyler Reddick has already won at Darlington this year. Now he's back, the points have reset, and he's sitting closer to the championship lead than his rough summer ever suggested he'd be.
Here's why Darlington could turn into the moment Reddick flips this entire championship on its head.
Reddick doesn't need to guess whether he can win at Darlington. He's already done it in 2026.
Back in March, Reddick won the Goodyear 400 at Darlington, and it wasn't a clean, easy win. He started from the pole, but early in the race his team dealt with an alternator problem serious enough to require a battery change, sending him to the back of the field. He had no cool suit for parts of the race and had to manage a brake issue on top of it. Despite all of that, he fought back to the front, took the lead with under 30 laps remaining, and held on to beat Brad Keselowski by nearly six seconds.
That win matters for one simple reason. Darlington is one of the hardest tracks on the schedule to master, and Reddick proved he could win there even when nothing about his day went according to plan. Now he returns for the Southern 500, the race that opens the Chase, at the same track where he's already shown he can handle adversity and still finish first.
To understand why Reddick is such a dangerous name heading into the postseason, you have to look at the season he's actually had, because it hasn't been one story. It's been two completely different ones.
Reddick opened 2026 as fast as anyone in the sport. He won the season-opening Daytona 500, then reeled off two more wins right after it, part of a stretch of five victories in the first nine races. He led the championship standings for the first 17 races of the season, and by mid-spring, his points lead had grown to 129, a number that looked nearly impossible to catch.
Then the second half of his season happened.
As the schedule turned toward summer, a string of on-track incidents and mechanical issues hit the No. 45 team hard. Denny Hamlin, his own teammate and co-owner at 23XI Racing, caught and passed him in the standings at Sonoma in June. Over Reddick's next 12 races, five of them ended in 30th place or worse. By the time the regular season wrapped up, Reddick had gone from a 129-point cushion to trailing Hamlin by 133 points.
Reddick himself compared the season to a coin, one side dominant, the other side rough, with no in-between. That kind of swing would break some teams. But it's also exactly why Darlington is such a dangerous spot for the rest of the field to face him.
Here's the part that makes Reddick such a threat right now: none of that 133-point deficit matters anymore.
NASCAR's Chase format reseeds the entire field once the playoffs begin. Regular-season points get wiped, and every driver starts the 10-race postseason on a leveled setup, with a fresh gap based on the new seeding. Under that reset, Reddick enters the Chase ranked third of the 16 drivers in the field, just 35 points behind Hamlin, the No. 1 seed.
Think about that. A driver who was bleeding points for three months, badly enough to fall over 130 points behind his own teammate, is now within striking distance of the championship lead again, at the exact track where he's already proven he can win under pressure.
That's not a small break. That's a driver who just got a clean restart at the worst point of his season, walking straight into the one racetrack on the calendar where his recent history says he shows up and wins.
This isn't just about one win in March. Reddick has built a real track record at Darlington in the Next Gen car. He's carried four consecutive top-10 finishes into this race, and he's recorded five top-three finishes at the track since 2022, more than any other active driver.
That consistency matters more at Darlington than almost anywhere else on the schedule. Known as "The Track Too Tough to Tame," Darlington's narrow, egg-shaped oval punishes small mistakes more severely than most tracks. Half of the last nine Cup races there featured a caution within the first six laps. The driver who leads the most laps has failed to win three of the last six Southern 500s. It's a track where surviving matters as much as speed, and Reddick has shown he can do both.
To be clear, Reddick isn't walking into an empty field. Hamlin enters as the No. 1 seed and the regular-season champion, backed by four wins, 14 top-fives, and a series-leading number of laps led. He's also a five-time Darlington winner, more than any other active driver at the track. Chase Briscoe, Reddick's Toyota teammate at Joe Gibbs Racing, is chasing something historic: a third consecutive Southern 500 win, a feat no one has managed since Jeff Gordon in the 1990s.
And Toyota as a manufacturer is loaded. Six Toyota drivers made the 16-driver Chase field, more than Chevrolet or Ford, and Toyota teams combined to win 14 of the season's 26 regular-season races. That means Reddick isn't just fighting Fords and Chevrolets for the win. He's fighting his own manufacturer stablemates, some of the strongest cars in the entire field.
But here's what separates Reddick from the pack. Hamlin has the points lead, but Reddick has the freshest proof that this specific track suits him right now. Briscoe has the recent Darlington history, but Reddick already beat him there once this season. No other driver in the field enters Sunday with both a 2026 win at this exact track and a stat sheet showing he's been one of the most consistent Darlington performers of the last several years.
If Reddick wins the Southern 500, it doesn't just hand him a trophy. It hands him playoff points that could carry through the rest of the 10-race Chase, and it closes the gap on Hamlin before the season even settles into a rhythm. For a field full of former champions and proven closers, an early statement win from Reddick would be the clearest signal yet that his rough summer is over, and that the driver who won five of the first nine races this year never actually went away.
The Chase always starts at the track that shows no mercy. This year, it also starts at the one track where Tyler Reddick has already proven he belongs at the front. If history repeats itself on Sunday, the rest of the championship field may be chasing him for the next nine races.
