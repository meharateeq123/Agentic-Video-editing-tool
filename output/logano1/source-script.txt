Joey Logano JUST Dropped a BOMBSHELL on Kyle Larson at Gateway Pole!

One lap. Two-tenths of a second. That's all it took for Joey Logano to send a message to the entire Chase field before Sunday's race even started. He didn't just beat Kyle Larson for the pole at Gateway. He beat him by one of the widest margins anyone has seen in years. Here's exactly how it happened, and why the timing of this pole could matter more than the pole itself.
Qualifying for the Enjoy Illinois 300 at World Wide Technology Raceway came down to a single flying lap from Joey Logano. Driving the No. 22 Team Penske Ford, Logano covered the 1.25-mile Gateway oval in 32.817 seconds, an average speed of 137.124 miles per hour. He was the only driver in the entire 36-car field to break the 33-second barrier. Nobody else even reached an average speed of 136 miles per hour.
That's not a narrow win. That's a gap. Logano beat second-place qualifier Kyle Larson, who ran 135.980 miles per hour, by 0.276 seconds. In a sport where qualifying sessions are often decided by hundredths of a second, a gap approaching three-tenths is enormous. It marked the largest margin for a Cup Series pole winner in recent memory.
For Logano personally, the lap carried extra weight. It was his 35th career pole, his second of the season, and his very first pole at Gateway, a track where he'd struggled specifically in qualifying despite running well on race day. "I feel like I've had good cars here for qualifying, I just have always screwed up Turn 1," Logano admitted afterward. "It's in your head, right? Before you go out, because you've had four qualifying sessions before where you've always screwed up Turn 1. It's nice to have a good, clean lap."
Poles matter every week, but this one landed at a particularly important moment for Logano. He enters Sunday's race eighth in the Chase standings, 72 points behind leader Denny Hamlin. After a slow start to the 2026 season, Logano has picked up two wins this year, but he was still looking for a signature weekend to close the points gap heading into the second round of the playoffs.
Logano didn't hide how much this specific track means to his championship push. "This is a racetrack that we had circled that we need to capitalize on, right? We know our points situation. We need to score max points here," he said. "And starting up front will help that for sure. Just gotta manage the strategy. Definitely got a fast car though. The Shell/Pennzoil Mustang in race trim was really good. Short run, long run, obviously, qualifying was spectacular."
That's a driver treating this pole as more than a nice starting spot. Starting first at Gateway means avoiding traffic in the opening laps, banking stage points early, and controlling the race on his own terms, all things Logano needs if he wants to climb back into real title contention.
There's also history working in his favor here. Logano won the very first Cup Series race ever held at Gateway back in 2022, and he's finished inside the top five in every single one of his four career starts at the track. A driver with that kind of track record, now starting from the pole, is not a name the rest of the Chase field wants to see at the front of the field on Sunday.
Kyle Larson wasn't far behind, but at Gateway, far behind still means starting alongside Logano on the front row. Larson, the defending two-time Cup Series champion, will roll off from the outside pole. He enters the race sitting seventh in the Chase standings, still searching for his first win in 50 races. A driver with that much speed and that much recent frustration starting second, right next to the fastest car in the field, sets up an immediate battle once the green flag drops.
Behind the front row, the next two spots went to a pair of drivers who know exactly what it takes to win right now. Ryan Blaney, who had actually led the way in Saturday's practice session, qualified third. Christopher Bell, fresh off winning the Chase-opening Southern 500 at Darlington, rounded out the second row in fourth. Two proven winners, both starting close enough to the front to make a run at Logano early if his car isn't as strong on long runs as it was for one qualifying lap.
Chase Briscoe started fifth, followed by Carson Hocevar in sixth, giving the top six spots to six different Chase-eligible drivers before a single lap of Sunday's race had even been run.
Denny Hamlin, the No. 1 seed and current points leader, qualified 10th. That's not a bad starting spot by any measure, but it does put him behind six of his direct championship rivals before the race even begins. Hamlin enters Sunday leading Bell by just 12 points in the standings, a gap that narrowed sharply after his own rough night at Darlington. Starting outside the top five means Hamlin will need to work through traffic in the opening laps if he wants to protect that lead rather than watch it shrink further.
A dominant qualifying lap is not the same as a race win, and Logano knows that better than most. Track position matters at Gateway, but so does long-run pace, pit strategy, and everything else that plays out over the course of 300 miles. Still, starting from the pole gives Logano control over exactly the kind of race he says he needs right now: clean air, an early shot at stage points, and a real chance to close the gap on Hamlin before the Chase moves any further.
The margin Logano posted in qualifying is the kind of number that gets a paddock's attention. Whether it translates into a race-winning car on Sunday is the question that actually decides this weekend. Larson, sitting right next to him on the front row, is more than capable of turning a five-tenths deficit in qualifying into a race lead within the first ten laps if his long-run speed shows up the way it did at Darlington. Blaney and Bell, both starting in the second row, have already proven this season they can win from exactly this kind of position.
For Logano, the pole is the first step in a weekend he's already said matters more than most. He circled this race. He needs the points. And for one lap on Saturday, he was faster than anyone else in the field by a margin nobody else came close to matching. Now the only question left is whether that speed shows up again on Sunday, when it actually counts.
