Ryan Blaney's BRAKES JUST DESTROYED His Championship Run!

Ryan Blaney was running fifth. Then, in the blink of an eye, a piece of his own car exploded through the hood. No crash. No mistake. Just one part, failing at the worst possible second. Here's what actually happened — and what it just cost him.
Blaney came into Sunday's Enjoy Illinois 300 at World Wide Technology Raceway looking like one of the strongest cars in the field. He posted back-to-back fourth-place finishes in Stage 1 and Stage 2, exactly the kind of consistent points he needs in a 10-race Chase. By the final stage, he was running sixth and closing in on Denny Hamlin, the points leader, for fifth place.
This wasn't a driver hanging on or scraping by. This was a driver in position to gain ground on the exact man he needed to catch.
On Lap 155 of the scheduled 240, crossing the start-finish line, the right-front brake rotor on Blaney's No. 12 Ford exploded, sending debris straight through the hood of his own car. The failure was instant and total. Blaney bounced off the outside wall entering Turn 1, his championship afternoon over in the span of a single corner.
He knew immediately how bad it was. "I have no brakes," Blaney radioed his team. "So, tell everyone to look out here."
That's not a driver managing a mechanical issue. That's a driver with no way to slow the car down, warning the rest of the field to stay out of his way while he limped it back to the garage. He eventually got the car off the racing surface and crawled it home, but there was no coming back from the damage. He was credited with a 35th-place finish and scored just 16 points on the day, a brutal outcome for a car that had been fast enough to run inside the top five all afternoon.
Blaney didn't dodge the reality of it afterward. "Didn't feel great about them the run before, and finally expired," he said of the brakes, describing warning signs he'd already noticed before the failure actually hit.
Here's what makes this failure even more frustrating for Blaney's team. He wasn't the only Chase contender to lose brakes on Sunday. Gateway has a history of brake problems dating back to when the Cup Series first raced there in 2022. The layout is the reason why: long straightaways feeding into tight corners force drivers to brake hard and repeatedly, pushing brake temperatures as high as 1,200 degrees Fahrenheit before they cool again on the next straight. That repeated heating and cooling puts massive stress on the components, and on Sunday, it broke more than one car.
Zane Smith suffered a brake failure earlier in the race that sent him hard into the wall, an impact serious enough that he needed help getting out of the car, though he was later checked and released. Chase Elliott, running right behind Blaney, watched the entire thing happen in front of him, and then suffered the exact same failure himself soon after.
"I was right behind Ryan when he blew his," Elliott said. "I guess it's kind of the luck of the draw, unfortunately. I just went to make a move on the No. 19 there. I stepped on the brakes, exploded a rotor, and that was about it."
Two championship contenders, in the same race, losing brakes within laps of each other. That's not one unlucky part. That's a pattern serious enough that it wiped out multiple title hopefuls on the same afternoon.
Before Sunday, Blaney sat third in the Chase standings, 16 points behind leader Denny Hamlin, well within range with eight races still to go. After the brake failure, that gap exploded. Blaney now trails Hamlin by 33 points, more than double what it was heading into the race.
To make it worse, this wasn't a static day at the top. Kyle Larson won the race, snapping his own 51-race winless streak, and vaulted from seventh to second in the standings, just nine points behind Hamlin. Christopher Bell, last week's Darlington winner, slipped to third after getting caught in a late-race incident. Blaney, meanwhile, fell further down the order, with a finish that erased almost all the ground his strong stage results had built earlier in the day.
In a championship format where one bad race can undo weeks of solid points, this is exactly the kind of result that changes the entire complexion of a title run. Blaney didn't lose ground because he made a mistake. He lost it because a part failed underneath him with no warning he could have acted on in time.
To his credit, Blaney didn't spiral into frustration publicly. Asked what could be done to prevent another failure, he was direct about the uncertainty his own team is facing. "We haven't had a failure in our company on the brakes here," Blaney said, "so we'll just have to look at if there was anything different, and I'll leave it up to the team."
His crew chief, Jonathan Hassler, tried to find the positive in a day that could have gone even worse. "Fortunately, we were in a decent spot with the points coming in," Hassler said. "We've got a lot of good tracks coming up, so we will move on and do the best we can every week."
Blaney's own words after the race carried the tone of a driver trying to move forward fast. "Just got to keep going," he said. "Go to the next week and try to have a good day. It's a shame. I thought we did a good job today. The car was good and had good stage points and was looking forward to the end of the race. We will just go race next week."
That's a driver refusing to let one race define his entire Chase. But the math doesn't lie. A 16-point gap just became a 33-point gap, and it happened in the exact kind of race where Blaney had done almost everything right until the moment his brakes gave out.
The Chase moves to Bristol Motor Speedway for the Bass Pro Shops Night Race on Saturday, September 19th, the third race of the 10-race postseason. All 16 drivers remain in contention for the title, which means Blaney's championship hopes are still alive. But the margin for error just got much smaller.
Bristol is a completely different animal than Gateway, a short, high-banked track where track position and car control matter more than outright speed. It's also a track that rewards drivers who can put a clean, mistake-free run together, exactly what Blaney needs after Sunday's disaster.
The question now isn't whether Blaney can still win the championship. Nine races remain, and 33 points is not an impossible number to make up. The real question is whether his team can find and fix whatever went wrong with those brakes before it happens again, and whether Blaney can put together the kind of bounce-back performance at Bristol that turns this setback into a footnote instead of the moment his title hopes quietly slipped away.
